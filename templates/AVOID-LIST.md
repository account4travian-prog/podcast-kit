# Avoid List (generic template)

The standard the watcher and auditor apply to every proposed turn.
Replace or extend these shapes with your own production's list.

## The deletion test
Delete the phrase. If the turn loses no fact, stance, pressure or
load-bearing image, the phrase fails. A compact phrase that IS the
sentence's main point (fronted, doing the work) passes; the same kind
of phrase trailing after the point was already made fails.

## Confirmed filler shapes (generic)
1. Virtue tag - a phrase that only signals the speaker is being
   careful, honest or rigorous, without adding a fact.
2. Herald - announcing a point instead of making it ("here is the
   thing that matters:").
3. Credo crown - ending a turn on a polished statement of principle
   the conversation did not ask for.
4. Standing announcement - declaring what you are about to do
   ("let me turn to...") instead of doing it.
5. Performed-authenticity adverb - "genuinely", "truly", "honestly"
   added only to show the speaker means it.
6. Elegant-variation flourish - renaming a thing a second time in
   fancier words for style alone.
7. Second-spend metaphor - an image repeated after it already did
   its work once in the room.
8. Act label - narrating what you just did ("- conceded", "- noted").
9. Echo - a formulation anyone already used in this conversation,
   restated as if new.

## Structural spent-shape
A turn architecture used more than twice by one speaker in a room is
spent, even if every instance passes the deletion test alone:
polished antithesis closers, verdict-then-argument, recap-then-question.

## Watcher checks beyond deletion
- Contingency: a turn that would be the same whatever came before it
  fails - the speaker is not listening.
- Read-aloud: object to missing contractions where natural, sentences
  stacked with clause breaks, and written-only syntax.
- Facts: only facts from the shared brief, the conversation, or the
  speaker's own field. Invented numbers, studies, quotes or people fail.
