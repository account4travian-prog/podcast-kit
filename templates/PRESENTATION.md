# Presentation Rules (generic template)

How lines are marked for the voice engine. Adapt the mark set to your
engine; the discipline matters more than the exact syntax.

## Plan delivery per sentence
People change pace and volume with how they feel about what they are
saying. Before writing a sentence, place its delivery:

  low + fast   = an aside, wry, "obviously"
  loud + fast  = impassioned, delighted, pressing
  low + slow   = weight, care, "hear this exactly"
  loud + slow  = insisting, ruling, final
  plain middle = most sentences; no mark needed

Move when the feeling moves. Do not stay in one cell for a whole room,
and two speakers should not share a cell for long.
Defects: a flat plan (a turn whose feeling shifts while delivery stays
untagged in one cell) and a stuck cell (a third consecutive turn in
the same cell without a content reason).

## Marks
- A delivery direction in square brackets BEFORE the sentence it
  changes: two to eight words, naming pace and/or volume and intent.
  At most one per sentence, only where delivery changes. Write your
  own for this sentence; a repeated direction is clutter.
- Span marks around one to five words, e.g. <emphasis>, <stress>,
  <soft>, <fast>, <slow>, <forceful>, <whisper>.
- Sound marks where they would really happen, e.g. [sighs], [laughs],
  [pause] - at most two per turn. [pause] only where the speaker
  actually stops to think; the engine already pauses at punctuation.

## Rules for all marks
- Marks are performance, never words: they carry no meaning the words
  do not. No content inside a mark.
- Honesty: a mark must match the line's meaning and the speaker's
  register (a laugh over something that is not funny is dishonest).
- The deletion test applies to marks too: a mark that changes nothing
  is clutter.
- Punctuation is prosody. Every comma, colon and dash becomes an
  audible pause, so punctuate the way you would say it. Contract the
  way you would say it.
